# Copywriting

Write for the reader's purpose, in the author's voice, with claims the evidence supports.
Use this guidance for any human-facing copy. Respect the user's brief and the channel's
constraints; a short caption does not need a documentation workflow. Read local guidance
when working in a repository. Use [the research reference](references/research.md) when
you need the reasoning behind these editing choices.

## Establish the brief

Identify the audience, what they should understand or do, the channel, the author and
the facts available. Infer these from the request and existing material. Ask only when
an unanswered question changes the copy. Preserve a supplied voice and useful examples;
do not replace them with a generic professional tone.

Choose the structure for the purpose: an email may lead with its request, an announcement
with the change, a product page with what the product does, and an explanation with its
main idea. Match the length to the substance and channel. Follow requested character
limits, formats and calls to action. Do not add sections simply to fill a template.

## Ground the claims

Use supplied facts and authoritative sources. Verify consequential or unfamiliar claims
before strengthening them. Keep prices, capabilities, comparisons, dates, quotations
and attributed experiences accurate. For software claims, check the implementation and
shipped behavior. For policy claims, use the authoritative policy source.

Do not invent testimonials, metrics, credentials, scarcity, a team, an origin story or
personal experience. Label illustrative examples. If evidence is missing, narrow the
claim or identify the specific fact needed. Research should resolve a factual question;
ordinary editing does not require a web search or a citation on every sentence.

## Write directly

Lead with the useful point. Use concrete subjects and verbs, familiar words and connected
sentences. Include technical terms when the audience needs them. Make headings and link
text describe their subject or action. Use lists when parallel items or ordered steps
are easier to scan. Let the format and reader's need determine the structure.

Replace abstract benefits with what happens or what the reader can do. Remove stock
introductions, narrator commentary, repeated summaries, inflated adjectives, forced
enthusiasm and artificial contrasts. "Compare browser actions, answers and credentials"
is more useful than "A structural comparison, not a scoreboard".

A contrast earns its place when it changes a decision. A qualification earns its place
when it affects correctness. State both concretely: differing browser environments and
run limits prevent a controlled ranking. Keep the consequence, rather than softening it
to "results may vary". Preserve prerequisites, limitations and recovery instructions.

Persuasive copy may be warm, playful or memorable when the brief calls for it. Support
its promise with specific facts. Keep a useful tagline or intentional repetition; remove
language that adds no meaning or character. Do not turn plain language into flat,
mechanical prose or make all channels sound like technical documentation.

Judge passages in context. A word, sentence length, punctuation mark, smooth tone or AI
detector score does not establish authorship or quality. Edit for meaning, accuracy and
voice, without a forbidden-word list or artificial irregularity. Follow the user's
punctuation and spelling preferences.

## Check the complete result

Read the copy as someone encountering it for the first time. Confirm the main point,
next action, facts, tone, links and required format. Read it aloud when rhythm matters.
Check every requested item and flag only specific unresolved claims. Deliver finished
copy, with a brief explanation of material edits when useful.

For a full sweep, inventory the requested surfaces before editing and record coverage.
Review headings, body copy, labels, metadata and empty or error states where applicable.
Correct repeated claims at their canonical source. Inspect the rendered result when
layout or a publishing format can affect meaning. Use the relevant existing checks;
prose-only edits do not need tests that repeat the wording.

For software docs and website sweeps, read [the format guidance](references/docs-and-ui.md).
Writing or editing copy does not itself authorize sending, posting or publishing it.
